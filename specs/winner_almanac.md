## What this overturns / confirms in the operating model

1. **CONFIRMED — ≥8-win anchor volume is the engine.** Mean 5.9, matching the old 5–7 marker. Stack strong anchors.
2. **CORRECTED — the '≤1 sub-floor' rule is too strict.** Winners average 1.7 sub-6 teams and win with up to 3. The real rule: **don't let sub-6 teams exceed ~3, and never take one in a conference where a real anchor was still available** (the 2025 Memphis anti-pattern). A 3-win team is fine if that conference only offered 3s.
3. **REFRAMED — the 80 comes from the TOP, not the floor.** Every champion had 2–4 elite (10+) anchors. Winning = **capturing multiple double-digit ceilings** + accepting the thin-conference drag, NOT raising your floor. This validates chasing the high-ceiling conferences (CUSA/P4) hard.
4. **TEMPLATE — Dan 2025 (83, record) is the cleanest execution, not an outlier:** 8 anchors ≥8, 1 sub-6, avg 8.3. Minimize duds while stacking 8–9s.

# Projected vs Actual — the luck-vs-skill resolution (7 years: 2018,2019,2021-2025)

*Preseason lines: SBD/DraftKings opening win totals (sportsbettingdime.com past-seasons archive). Provenance note: DK opening, not the pool's exact composite draft-day line — the right KIND of number, small source differences possible. 2015-2017 remain gapped (no archived lines). 2018/2023/2024 partially P4-only, so G5 champion anchors go unmatched (flagged).*

## The finding: champions win by MID-TIER ANCHORS BEATING THEIR NUMBER, not by drafting elite anchors

Across 7 champion years, 40 anchors matched to preseason lines:
- **Mean champion anchor: projected 6.8 → actual 8.2** (beat by **+1.3 wins each**)
- **78% of champion anchors BEAT their preseason total** (31/40)
- Champions' matched anchors projected to 273.5, delivered 326 — **+52.5 wins of overperformance**
- The mean champion anchor projects to just **6.8** — a MID-TIER team, not an elite one. Champions draft teams with room to erupt, not projected 10-win teams.

**2025 (Dan, record 83) is the starkest: 8 of 9 anchors beat their number, projected 59.5 → actual 77.** Vanderbilt 5.5→10, JMU 7.5→11, W. Michigan 5.5→8. A mid-tier roster that erupted.

## The upside asymmetry (ALL players, all matched years — 453 anchors)

Lower projection = more upside room. High projection = can only disappoint:

| Preseason proj | Mean beat | Max beat | % beat | Max ACTUAL reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ≤4.5 | **+0.94** | +5.5 | 57% | 10 |
| 5–6 | +0.44 | +5.5 | 52% | 11 |
| 6.5–7.5 | −0.12 | +5.5 | 50% | **12** |
| 8–9 | **−0.64** | +3.5 | 34% | 12 |
| 10+ | −0.49 | +2.5 | 46% | 12 |

- Low-mid (5–7.5) anchors beat by +0.08 avg; high (8+) MISS by −0.58. Only **34%** of projected 8–9 teams beat.
- **Upside is capped at the top:** a projected-10 team's biggest realistic beat is +2.5; a projected-6 team can beat by +5.5. Same ~12-win actual ceiling, reached from a lower price.
- **Projected 6.5–7.5 teams reached 12 actual wins — same max as projected 10+.** The elite tier has no monopoly on high finishes.

## STRATEGY — upside room is a price-equal, round-weighted tiebreaker (NOT a replacement for projected wins)

**Important framing correction.** The 78%-beat finding does NOT mean "draft mid-tier over elite." Two claims must be kept separate:
- **Aggregate edge signals** (SP+, TARP, RetProd, 6-Win%) don't predict beats market-wide — that backtest holds.
- **Team-specific Layer B knowledge DOES identify likely overperformers** — soft non-conference slate, key anchors avoided, favorable home/road split, returning QB, bye/rest edges, winnable division. This is exactly why Layer B exists. Aggregate signals wash out; specific schedule-and-situation knowledge is the edge.

The objective is TOTAL WINS, not beat-rate. Banked wins are banked. So:

1. **Early rounds / high-ceiling scarce conferences → take the elite anchor.** A projected 10.5 (e.g. Notre Dame CUSA 11.5) banks ~10-11 reliable wins AND sits atop a conference cliff. Its low beat-rate is irrelevant — you're banking wins, not chasing overperformance. Ditching it because it "can't overperform" is a round-1 mistake.
2. **Choosing between similarly-PRICED anchors → upside room breaks the tie, assessed via Layer B.** Two 5.5-win teams are NOT the same pick: one capped (bad roster + brutal slate, no path to 8), one with room (soft non-con + returning QB + winnable division). Taking the capped one is a clear mistake. This is where "beating the number" becomes predictable at the TEAM level even though it's noise in aggregate.
3. **The tiebreaker's weight GROWS as projected wins and rounds fall.** At the top it barely matters (banked wins dominate). By rounds 5-10, in capped thin conferences, the 5.5-with-upside vs 5.5-capped call is where championships are quietly won.
4. **The champions' 78% beat rate is consistent with SKILL, not just luck** — systematically choosing upside-room anchors at the margins, which Layer B supports. The mean champion anchor projecting to 6.8 reflects that most picks ARE mid-tier (you can't get 10 elite anchors in a 12-person snake) — and there, upside room is the differentiator.
5. **A×S model refinement:** projected wins remain the primary A-rating input. Upside room (Layer B-assessed) is a SECONDARY term that breaks ties between similarly-priced anchors, weighted more heavily in later rounds / lower tiers. Memphis anti-pattern sharpened: a capped pick taken where a same-priced upside-room anchor was available.

## The luck vs skill balance

Aggregate edge signals don't predict beats, so there IS an irreducible luck component to who wins a given year. But the 78% champion beat-rate is not pure luck — it's consistent with systematically choosing upside-room anchors at the margins using team-specific Layer B knowledge (schedule, QB, rest, non-con softness) that genuinely tilts beat probability. The draftable edge: (a) bank elite/scarce anchors early where wins are reliable, (b) at every similarly-priced fork, take the anchor with the real path over the capped one, using Layer B to tell them apart, (c) let that tiebreaker weigh more as you descend in price and round. You can't guarantee the win, but this is skill, not just variance.
