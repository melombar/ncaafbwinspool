# Pre-Pick Doctrine — run at EVERY pick / ranking / anchor-value decision

*This exists because the nuanced rule keeps getting flattened into a quotable slogan that drops its conditions. This doctrine predicts that failure and catches it. Read it before any pick call.*


## THE TRIAD — three things hold at once, none dropped (item 7 guardrail)
Claude's failure mode is dropping two of three when it grabs one. All THREE are always live:
1. **SCARCITY** — few usable teams exist per conference; usable supply (anchors AND upside mid-tiers) drains over the draft. Governs WHEN you must act on a conference (depth-countdown to ≤2-3 usable). NEVER dropped just because upside is interesting.
2. **FLOOR** — a pick below the ~6.0 usable line actively sinks the roster (you need to average 8.0 to hit 80). Winners tolerate 1.7 sub-6 teams (up to 3) but never PILE them, and never take a sub-6 where a real anchor was available. The floor still matters.
3. **UPSIDE AVAILABILITY** — the +2-win-room teams you're counting on must be ON THE BOARD at your pick. Knowing which teams have upside is worthless if they've drained. Upside is chosen WITHIN scarcity, above the floor.
If a pick argument mentions one of these and not the other two, STOP — you're flattening. The right frame weighs all three: "this conference is scarce (act now), this pick clears the floor, and among the available it has the most upside room."

## THE OBJECTIVE (never subordinate to any rule below)
**Maximize expected TOTAL regular-season wins across the final 10-anchor roster.**
Not beat-rate. Not floor. Not ceiling. Not upside. Not consistency. Not balance. Not G5-ness. Not scarcity. **Those are all PROXIES.** A pick with a "bad" proxy value can be correct if it maximizes expected total wins.

## THE FLATTENING TRAP (the failure mode to catch — name it out loud)
Every heuristic below is **conditional**. The recurring failure is **promoting a conditional heuristic to an unconditional rule**:
- "draft for upside, not ceiling" ← FALSE early / for scarce elite anchors
- "avoid sub-floor teams" ← FALSE; winners carry 1.7, up to 3
- "take G5 / thin conferences early" ← FALSE; the driver is anchor premium, not the label
- "high floor wins" ← FALSE; safe = capped = worst beat bucket
- "chase upside variance" ← FALSE as a blanket; it's a price-equal tiebreaker

**If you catch yourself applying a clean directional rule ("prefer X over Y") without checking round, price, and scarcity — THAT is the flattening. Stop and re-run the check.**

## THE CHECK (one line, every pick)
> "Does this pick maximize expected total wins **given price, scarcity, and round** — or am I optimizing a proxy?"

## THE CONDITIONS that flip the heuristics
- **Round / price:** elite scarce anchors win EARLY — bank reliable wins; their low beat-rate is irrelevant (you draft for total wins, not beat-rate). A reliable 10.5 in round 1 is correct even though it "can't overperform." Upside-room is a TIEBREAKER whose weight grows only as price and round fall.
- **Scarcity:** spend leverage where the cross-conference drop-off is steepest — not where a label (G5, thin, deep) says to.
- **Price-equal fork:** between similarly-priced anchors, Layer B (schedule tag, key avoids/draws, QB, rest/bye, non-con softness, host lean, **game count** — a 13th game is an extra win chance) breaks the tie toward the real path over the capped one. By rounds 5–10 this is the primary differentiator and where championships are quietly won.
- **Scarcity is the precondition, not a competitor.** Upside room operates WITHIN the scarcity-timed pick. If you're counting on an overperforming 5-6 win team, it must still be on the board at your pick — in a bottom-heavy conference the upside mid-tiers drain too, so you can't punt a thin conference expecting to grab the overperformer later. S1-S5 tells you WHEN supply is gone; upside tells you WHICH of the available to prefer. Never throw out the scarcity/anchor-premium doctrine (memory #2, #7).
- **Pool conf ≠ real schedule.** Never apply a pool conference's schedule structure to its reassigned members. Reassigned P4 teams play their REAL partial-round-robin schedules (real avoids/draws = real schedule upside), captured from their REAL conference's pod, not the pool-conf pod. (I made this exact error: called the whole pool Pac-12 a round-robin when only the 8 real MW/Pac-2 teams are — the 4 reassigned P4 teams have full schedule-avoidance signal.)

## PROXY vs OBJECTIVE — the tell
| Proxy (subordinate, conditional) | The objective |
|---|---|
| beat-rate, upside, floor, ceiling, consistency, balance, G5-membership, scarcity-count | expected total wins |

Any sentence of the form "always prefer [proxy]" is suspect. The correct form is "given [round/price/scarcity], the pick that maximizes expected total wins is ___, and here upside/floor/scarcity matters because ___."

## PAIRING PRINCIPLE (for memory & docs)
**Never record a finding without its guardrail in the same breath.** "Champions beat their number 78%" must always travel with "— which reflects team-specific Layer B skill at the margins, NOT 'draft mid-tier over elite'; elite anchors still win early." A finding stored alone will be flattened.
